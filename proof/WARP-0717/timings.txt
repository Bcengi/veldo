THE FULL RUN IS ALSO THE POSITIVE CONTROL FOR THE FLAG REFUSAL REVIEW 1's C3 ADDS. It carries
no argument at all, so it is the shape a new argument check could most easily have broken:
exit 0 and the aggregate summary line, unchanged. The other two recognised shapes are proven
unchanged in proof/WARP-0717/flag-shapes.txt, where `--list` is byte-identical and both
space-form selectors are identical with elapsed times masked.

WHAT THESE NUMBERS SAY, INCLUDING THE PART THAT IS NOT GOOD NEWS.
The saving is real but it is NOT uniform, because the suite's cost is concentrated rather
than spread. Fragment 02 is 15.02s and fragment 12 is 46.57s of the 94.07s the 13 pre-existing
fragments take together. The closure excludes 02 for 11 of the 13 and excludes 12 for all but
12 itself, and that is the entire source of every second saved. So an engineer iterating on a
small fragment gets a genuine inner loop (0.07s for fragment 05 against 21.84s for the
equivalent --upto). An engineer iterating on fragment 12 still pays 59.80s, because half the
suite's cost is inside the thing being edited. Making THAT fast is a separate item about what
fragment 12 spends its time on.

Note the union case: `--suite 05,09` costs the same as `--suite 09` alone, because 09's
closure already contains 05. The union is a set union of closures, not a concatenation.

Note the `--upto 13` case, which is new this round and does a job beyond timing: it runs every
fragment EXCEPT this item's own and reports 3362 passed, the exact figure the base commit's
full run reported. With the full run at 3389 that pins this item's entire assertion delta
inside fragment 14, using the shipped selector rather than a second instrument.
