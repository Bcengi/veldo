# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be straight with people and assume they are being straight with you. Argue with the work, not the
person who brought it. Say what you mean plainly, including when the answer is no.

This project is unusually blunt about defects: reviews name what is wrong, findings become permanent
checks, and a claim that turns out to be false gets deleted rather than reworded. **That bluntness is
aimed at artifacts, never at people.** "This check cannot fail" is a finding about a check. "You are
careless" is not a finding about anything.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or comments about someone's identity or characteristics.
- Sexual attention or imagery, anywhere in the project or its channels.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained derailing, or treating a review as a contest to be won.
- Pressuring anyone to approve, verify, or vouch for something they have not examined. In this
  project a verdict carries a person's name, and nobody should be pushed into signing one.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere someone is representing the
project.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on this repository
(Security tab, Report a vulnerability), which reaches the maintainers and nobody else. Use it for
conduct as well as for security; it is the private channel that exists.

Reports are read by the maintainers. A report about a maintainer is handled by a maintainer who is not
the subject of it. You will get an acknowledgement within three working days.

## Consequences

The response is proportionate and it is a person's judgement, not a formula: a private word, a
required edit, a temporary block from a thread, or removal from the project. Anyone who retaliates
against someone for reporting in good faith is removed.

We would rather resolve something with one honest conversation than with a process. Ask for that first
if you think it will work.
